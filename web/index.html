Relays

Each rule bridges a TCP port on the LAN to one BLE device: what a client sends goes to the device, what the device sends goes to every client.

Device
Scan listens for the BLE devices nearby. Probe connects to one, reads its services, characteristics and what it tells about itself (name, maker, model), and writes nothing; picking a device fills its address, its name and the service it advertises, and probes it unless that was done. The preset fills the HF-LPT270's service and characteristics.

Six hex bytes with colons. Empty: the relay connects to the strongest device that advertises the name, and the log warns when more than one does.
As the device advertises it. Enough without an address; with one it only labels the device.
Service
The GATT service that holds TX and RX. After a probe, the device's services; otherwise the UUID, 16-bit, 32-bit or 128-bit with dashes.
TX: TCP to BLE
With response the device confirms each write before the next goes out; without is faster, and a write the device cannot take is lost. The TCP clients see no difference.
RX: BLE to TCP
Indicate: the relay confirms each value; notify: unconfirmed. By properties: indicate when offered, notify otherwise.
Connection
On demand: with the first TCP client, until the idle time after the last one left. Always: while the relay is enabled.
TCP data longer than one write, the negotiated MTU - 3 bytes (the relay asks for 517, the most): chunk splits it into writes, long write sends it as one prepared write, drop discards it.

Other

Every TCP to BLE write and BLE to TCP value in the log as hex; fills the log fast.
FED4 is the HF-LPT270's AT-command characteristic: bytes written there are AT commands and can hang the module until a power cycle. Its data channel is TX FED5, RX FED6.
